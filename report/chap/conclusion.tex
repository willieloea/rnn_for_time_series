\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This section draws together the main findings, their practical implications
and directions for further work.

This study compared three recurrent neural network architectures across five
time-series forecasting problems. The shared pipeline provided a consistent way
to prepare data, select model settings and evaluate predictions on later
observations. The experiments demonstrate that forecasting performance depends
on the dataset and evaluation conditions, rather than on feedback structure
alone.

No architecture was uniformly best. Although the networks improved on
persistence in mean RMSE for four datasets, persistence performed better on the
airline-passenger test period (Fig.~\ref{fig:final-rmse}). Several differences
between architectures were small, so their numerical rankings should not be
treated as conclusive evidence of superiority. These findings highlight the
importance of simple baselines and careful interpretation of results.

The conclusions are limited to the tested configurations, data periods and
one-step forecast horizon. Further work could examine stronger baselines, longer
horizons and additional evaluation periods. For embedded applications, practical
measurements of memory use, latency and energy would also be needed. The main
lesson is that selecting a forecasting model requires considering accuracy,
complexity and the intended use together.
